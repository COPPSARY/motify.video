Why storyboard first?

Fix the story while it is still easy to change.

A prompt can sound clear while leaving the visual sequence unresolved. A storyboard makes the opening, product reveal, proof, pacing, and closing frame visible before you commit to a full video.

That gives founders, marketers, and product teams one shared artifact to review. You can spot a missing product moment, repeated scene, weak transition, or unclear CTA before those problems become expensive revisions.

Four-step workflow

From rough idea to approved visual direction.

  1. 01

    Describe the complete story

    Explain the audience, product, problem, transformation, proof, brand mood, and final action—not just a single shot.

  2. 02

    Choose the primary format

    Start with the channel that matters most so composition, framing, and text placement are planned for the right canvas.

  3. 03

    Review all nine scenes together

    Check the flow from problem to reveal to payoff. Make sure every scene adds new information and the product appears at the right moment.

  4. 04

    Approve and use it as a reference

    Carry the approved storyboard into your next Motify video prompt so generation begins from an agreed visual narrative.

Prompt pattern

Describe a journey, not a collection of shots.

Create a nine-scene launch story for a SaaS analytics dashboard. Begin with a marketing team overwhelmed by disconnected reports, reveal a single live dashboard, show three moments of faster decision-making, build toward a confident product hero shot, and close with an invitation to start a free workspace. Keep the direction precise, modern, and grounded in the supplied brand.
